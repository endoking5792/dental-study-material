## Preface to the Second Edition

Dear reader,

With the second edition of Molar Endodontics, you may ask yourself why do we need another textbook on endodontics? This is a valid question. In my view, the purpose of this book differs from a typical endodontic textbook and hopefully its range becomes apparent when you read it. The second edition extends the scope with two new chapters, and these are timely and relevant in today’s endodontic practice. The remaining chapters were updated and in part rewritten where appropriate.

Historically, the intention of a guidebook, according to several dictionaries that I consulted, was to give information to travelers. Therefore, regardless of where you are on your professional journey, a seasoned clinician, or a beginner, you may want to stay on the safe side of the road or perhaps you are keen to travel deeper into molar endodontics. This book intends to give you future-proof support in your decision-­making and execution of your treatment plan, specifically for the complex field of molar endodontics.

The concept for this book as well as its further development directly relates to several pertinent findings and discussions with patients as well as clinicians, endodontists, postgrads, general dentists, and fellow faculty members.

For example:

•	 Do we teach molar endodontics sufficiently to dental students or do we need

additional training to perform various procedures under this umbrella term?
•	 Do technological developments, such as the use of the operating microscope or

cone beam tomography (CBCT) or new avenues such as dental magnetic resonance tomography (MRI), lead to more successful molar endodontics?
•	 Is the need to avoid a gross mistake as important as doing little things right for

success?

The latter question came to the forefront when I began to consider the idea of risk as it relates to oral conditions and endodontic procedures. For this I have taken a closer look at the psychology of decision-making [1] and specific ways to mitigate risk. While it would be dangerous to oversimplify endodontics, it surely helps to step back occasionally and make sure that the right thing is done and that it is done

right. Better yet, one would like to use clinical evidence along with the consumer perspective whenever available to support the course of action [2].

Accordingly, each chapter in this book has a number of references but one key citation for a guiding principle intended to address a clinical quandary. With this comes the need to re-evaluate the standard of practice periodically, to include new and pertinent information.

Completing any book, and certainly one that requires broad clinical expertise, would be an insurmountable task if it was not for my co-authors that were each responsible for a chapter in this book; I cannot thank each of them enough!

From a clinical standpoint there are several key principles than can be cited for procedural success in root canal therapy. The phrase “procedural success” is in my view related to what the clinician can do to promote best patient-related outcomes.

As alluded to before, it is my impression that outcomes in endodontics will be discussed more broadly beyond the treatment of apical periodontitis; this helps not to primarily focus on radiographic resolution of apical bone defects but considers other outcomes that are relevant for our patients.

With evidence that the prevalence of apical periodontitis globally is as high as
50%, obviously depending on the selected detection tool [3], I would respectfully submit that endodontics must be dedicated to a notion of lifelong management rather than treatment of disease.

In a time where CBCT and MRI both permit real-time imaging of apical conditions, without clearly defining what a normal periradicular space looks like, an assessment of our strategies is in order. Evidently, approaches in dentistry vary from country to country, continent to continent, likely based less on high-level clinical evidence, but in fact on fee schedules and educational systems. This prompted me to solicit the help of well-educated clinician-researchers for each of the central issues discussed in this book, ranging from molar anatomy over diagnosis and clinical treatment to outcome assessment, retreatment, and surgery. My experience during the writing of the recently published S3 Guidelines [4] also strongly formed my view that endodontics needs a refresher as much as this textbook needed one!

It is my hope that you will enjoy this text as much as the supporting material in bullet points and images and, ultimately, enjoy traveling the road to successful molar endodontics!
