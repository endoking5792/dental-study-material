25.7 IDENTIFICATION OF OPIOID PEPTIDES IN PULP

We have already found using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) that three kinds of opioid peptides, Met-enk, Leu-enk and Metenk-Arg-Phe, are present in dental pulp. The content of these opioid pep tides increased remarkably in response to cavity formation in rat incisors. Specifically, the content of Met-enk, Leu-enk and Met-enk-ArgPhe increased two to six times as compared to that of the control pulp.
This fact indicated that the increase of Met-enk-like peptides was linked to the increase of precursor protein, Met-enk-Arg-Phe, which coexists iri the dental pulp much like Leu-enk and its precursor.

### 25.10 EFFECT OF OPIOIDS ON THE MET-ENK-LIKE PEPTIDE

### IN PULP

We have already mentioned that a bradykinin-like substance was released into the superfusate pooled on the exposed mandibular canine pulp of the dog following direct electrical stimulation (Inoki et al., 1973).
We also clarified that this release was clearly inhibited by pretreatment with morphine, and that this inhibition occurred even after the severance of the inferior alveolar nerve. Furthermore, we mentioned that the release of bradykinin was restored by the administration of narcotic antagonists such as nalorphine, levallorphan and naloxone. Based on these findings, we concluded that an opioid receptor exists in dental pulp tissue. Accordingly, we further investigated the opioid receptor subtype using in vivo experiments. When a cavity was formed after the pre-administration of morphine, which is a fL-subtype agonist, the increase in the bradykinin-like substance was moderately inhibited. A more effective inhibition was observed when ethylketocyclazocine, a
K-subtype agonist, was administered, and the effect was remarkably restored by Win 44,441-3, a K-subtype antagonist.

### 25.11 POSSIBLE PHYSIOLOGICAL ROLE OF OPIOID

### PEPTIDES IN DENTAL PULP

Many researchers have demonstrated peripheral actions of opioid peptides. We demonstrated that, in pulp, opioids and their precursors increased in response to bradykinin and to noxious stimulation, such as cavity formation. Tanzer and Desiderio (1981) reported that Met-enk,
Leu-enk and dynorphin are present in dog canine pulp. Met-enk, as well as Leu-enk, showed properties of the o-subtype agonist, and Metenk-Arg-Phe and dynorphin showed strong agonistic properties similar to the K- rather than the fL-receptor subtype. The K-subtype opioid receptor, which is abundant in blood vessels, is involved in the inhibition of inflammation. Opioid peptides found in dental pulp may playa role as antinociceptive and anti-inflammatory substances, reducing the effects of noxious stimulation. In other words, opioid peptides in dental pulp are important substances in the negative feedback mechanism which counteracts the formation of bradykinin, which is one sequence of initial tissue reactions resulting in pain or inflammation in dental pulp.

### 26.1 INTRODUCTION

One of the recent developments which made great contribution to the progress in biology and medicine is the discovery of a family of arachidonic acid metabolites such as prostaglandins (PGs) and leukotrienes (LTs). These metabolites, known as a family of 'eicosanoids', are formed in a variety of cells and tissues in response to specific or nonspecific stimuli, and have a number of potent biological activities which affect cardiovascular, renal, pulmonary, gastrointestinal and eye functions, platelet aggregation, peripheral and central nervous systems, and the reproductive system. Furthermore, these eicosanoids have been implicated in many aspects of the inflammatory process, including vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, pain, leukocyte migration and bone resorption.
Although the physiological and pathological roles of arachidonic acid metabolites have been extensively studied in various tissues and organs, less attention has been given to their roles in oral tissues. Particularly, such studies in pulp tissues have been hampered, apparently because of its anatomical location and relatively small size. However, the possible role of these metabolites in the pathogenesis of pulpal diseases ha,s been emphasized (Torabinejad and Bakland, 1980), and evidence is accumulating that indicates the presence of a functioning system for eicosanoid pr.oduction in the dental pulp. In the present chapter, based on more recent data from this and other laboratories, we will discuss the possible roles of arachidonic acid metabolites in the physiological functions and pathological conditions of the dental pulp.

### 26.2 PATHWAYS OF ARACHIDONIC ACID METABOLISM

Prostaglandins and LTs are derived from C:20 polyunsaturated fatty acids such as arachidonic acid. The precursor is released from membrane phospholipids when cells are stimulated by various mechanical, pharmamacological, physiological or pathological stimuli. Two main pathways have been suggested for the release of arachidonic acid. One is direct and involves the stimulation of phospholipase A2 as a rate-limiting step (Vogt, 1978); the other is indirect and involves a phosphatidylinositolspecific phospholipase C followed by a phosphatidic acid-specific phospholipase A2 (Billah et al., 1981) or di- and monoacyglycerollipases (Bell et al., 1979; Prescott and Majerus, 1983).
Arachidonic acid liberated from membrane phospholipids is oxygenated enzymatically by two pathways. One is the cyclooxygenase pathway by which arachidonic acid is converted to unstable cyclic endoperoxide intermediates, PGG2 and then PGH2 (Hamberg et al., 1974). These endoperoxides can be acted upon by a variety of enzymes, leading to a number of different products, depending upon the tissue and cells in which these are generated. Thromboxane (TX) synthase is responsible for the conversion of these endoperoxides to the short-lived substance
TXA2, which is rapidly converted to an inactive product, TXB2. TXA2 has potent activity in platelet aggregation, and is synthesized in a number of tissues, including platelets (Hamburg et al., 1975) and polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNLs; Higgs et al., 1976). In vascular tissue, PGIz (prostacyclin), another very short-lived PG that is rapidly converted to inactive 6-keto-PGF1", is the major product converted from
PG endoperoxides, which reaction is catalysed by prostacyclin synthase (Moncada et al., 1976). The PG endoperoxides can also be converted to
12-hydroxy-5,8,10-heptadecatrienoic acid (HHT) and malondialdehyde (MDA), and to the primary PGs such as PGE2, PGD2 and PGFz...
An alternative pathway of arachidonic acid metabolism is the lipoxygenase pathway, which leads to the formation of various non-cyclized hydroxy acids. 12-Lipoxygenase transforms arachidonic acid into an unstable hydroperoxy intermediate; 12-hydroperoxy-eicosatetraenoic acid (12-HPETE), which is subsequently converted to 12-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid (12-HETE; Nugteren, 1975). 5-Lipoxygenase, originally found in PMNLs (Borgeat et al., 1976), gives rise to an important family of metabolites containing a conjugated triene structure, named leukotrienes (LTs). The 5-lipoxygenase converts arachidonic acid into 5HPETE, which is either further reduced to 5-HETE or metabolized to the unstable 5,6-epoxide intermediate LTA4, the intermediate in the formation of a very potent chemokinetic agent, LTB4 (Ford-Hutchinson et al., 1980). ~HARMACOLOGICAL AGENTS

The activity of phospholipase A2, an initial and rate-limiting step in eicosanoid formation, is dependent on the presence of calcium (Vogt,
1978). The stimulation of rat pulp tissue by the calcium ionophore A23187 results in the increase of both PGh and TXA2 production (Hirafuji and
Ogura, 1983a) as well as the primary PGs (Hirafuji and Ogura, 1983b).
Interestingly, among some vasoactive substances tested, 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT, serotonin) causes a dose-dependent and remarkable stimulation of the release of PGE2 and PGh, but had no effect on the release of TXA2 (Hirafuji et al., 1982; Hirafuji and Ogura, 1987). Neither histamine nor bradykinin has such an effect. 5-HT-induced response, since, among 5-HT-related indolealkylamines tested, only tryptamine which interacts with 5-HT receptors (Frankhuijzen and Bonta, 1974) had a similar effect to 5-HT. 5-HT also induces

It may have pathophysiological relevances since it is conceivable that a high concentration of 5-HT released from aggregating platelets could be reached locally at the vicinity of the blood vessel wall when platelets are activated by vascular injury.
Platelet-activating factor (PAF-acether; 1-0-alkyl-2-acetyl-sn-glycero-3phosphocholine) is a newly discovered lipid mediator with diverse and potent biological activities suggesting an important role in a variety of pathophysiological conditions, including arterial thrombosis, acute inflammation, endotoxic shock and acute allergic diseases (Benveniste and Pretolani, 1985). This mediator is synthesized upon immunological or non-immunological stimulations in a variety of cell types such as basophils, neutrophils, eosinophils, macrophages, monocytes, platelets and vascular endothelial cells (Snyder, 1985).
More recently, we found that PAF-acether stimulated both PGrz and
TXA2 biosynthesis in rat dental pulp (Hirafuji and Ogura, 1988a). Lyso
P AF-acether, the deacetylated and biologically inactive metabolite, had no effect. The stimulatory effect of P AF-acether was dose-dependent, and inhibited by the specific antagonists, suggesting the specific receptor-mediated response. Since the effect on PGrz formation was completely inhibited when extracellular calcium was absent, while the effect on TXA2 formation was not, a different mechanism may be involved between PGrz and TXA2 production. This result also suggests again that cell types responsible for the production of these metabolites are different. The pathophysiological significance of the stimulatory effect of P AF-acether in the dental pulp remains to be clarified.

result in inflammation. Human dental pulps demonstrating inflammation have significantly higher contents of PGE2 than do the unstimulated pulps, and painful pulps have a much higher level of both PGE2 and
PGF2a than the asymptomatic pulps (Cohen et al., 1985). The endogenous levels of 6-keto-PGF1a, TXB2 and LTC4, and the rate of synthesis of these metabolites, are increased in experimentally induced inflammation of dog dental pulp (Lessard et al., 1986). When rat dental pulp is inflamed by applying bacterial lipopolysaccharide, the production of all arachidonic acid metabolites in tissue homogenates is increased (Okiji et al., 1987). These demonstrations that pulp inflammation is accompanied by an increased production of eicosanoids suggest their important roles in the pulp inflammatory process.
Colchicine, an anti-microtubular agent which causes disruption of microtubular structure and function, has been shown to cause severe histological changes of pulp tissue, including odontoblasts, as well as to interfere with dentine formation in rat incisors (Nogueira et al., 1980,
1981; Senzaki, 1980). According to these kinetic studies, histological changes in rat pulp tissue following a single injection of colchicine appears to be a typical process of inflammation in injured tissues.
We have found that a 24-hour treatment with colchicine causes an increased production of PGh and TXA2 by rat dental pulp tissue (Hirafuji and Ogura, 1988b). The stimulatory effect of colchicine was more marked on PGh than on TXA2 synthesis. This different effectiveness suggests again that PGh and TXA2 released from pulp tissue originate from different cell types, and hence these cells may have different sensitivity to colchicine. Since colchicine administration in a dose which caused a marked stimulation of PGh release from pulp tissues had no influence on PGh release from aortic tissue, the effect of colchicine on the pulp tissue seems to be local and selective. This enhancing effect is not due to the direct action on enzymes involved in the biosynthetic pathway of PGI2 and TXA2, because colchicine did not stimulate the release of PGI2 and TXA2 when incubated with the pulp tissue.
The kinetic studies revealed that, after a lag period of more than 12 hours, a single injection of colchicine caused a progressive increase in
PGh and TXA2 production, which reached a maximum at 24 hours and decreased thereafter gradually to the basal level within 168 hours (7 days). These kinetics of changes in PG and TX production are well correlated with those of histological changes in the dental pulp of colchicine-treated rats. Therefore, it is likely that the increase in PGI2 and TXA2 production by dental pulp in colchicine-treated rats is an inflammatory response to the tissue injury evoked by colchicine.

### 26.5.2 Involvement in the mechanism of dental pain

Dental pain represents a diagnostic symptom of an underlying inflammatory condition in the dental pulp. The involvement of PGs in the mechanism of dental pain has been of particular interest since the discovery that non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as aspirin inhibit PG synthesis (Vane, 1971), because most dental pain can be well controlled by NSAIDs. Indeed, these drugs have been shown to inhibit PG synthesis by pulp tissues when evaluated in in vitro systems (Hirafuji et al., 1980; Antila and Pohto, 1984; Lessard et al., 1986) and applied systemically (Antila, 1985). Prostaglandins do not produce overt pain, but strongly increase the pain-producing activity of algesic substances such as bradykinin, histamine or 5-HT (Moncada et al., 1978).
Therefore, PGs should be considered as 'hyperalgesic mediators' rather than 'pain mediators', and the analgesic action of NSAIDs can be explained by inhibition of the hyperalgesic effect (Moncada et al., 1978). According to the rat paw hyperalgesia test, the hyperalgesia induced by PGEz has a delayed onset lasting more than 3 hours, whereas PGIz induces an early plateau (reached within 15-20 minutes) and a relatively short-lived hyperalgesia that disappears within 1 hour (Ferreira et al., 1978). The difference in the duration of the effects of PGEz and PGIz might explain the effectiveness of NSAIDs in the clinical situation. In most dental pain, the prompt analgesic action of NSAIDs would indicate the predominant participation of PGIz, probably generated by injured blood vessels.
In clinical practice, phenolic compounds such as eugenol and guaiacol are favourably used as topical medicaments in the treatment of pulp inflammatory pain because of their rapid and direct analgesic properties.
This effect could be explained, at least in part, by their ability to strongly inhibit PGIz formation in the dental pulp (Hirafuji, 1984). This aspect of the mechanism of action for these compounds is discussed in detail by
Professor Tsujimoto and co-workers elsewhere in this book. These drugs also depress intradental nerve activity (Trowbridge et al., 1982), an effect enhancing further their appeal in the dental therapy.
There is also some evidence that lip oxygenase products may playa role in inflammatory pain. Among LTs, only LTB4 reduces the latency for the development of yeast-induced hyperalgesia (Rackham and FordHutchinson, 1983) or induces hyperalgesia (Levine et al., 1984) in the rat hindpaw. PAF-acether has been shown to produce hyperalgesia when injected subcutaneously into the rat hindpaw, but this effect seems to be an indirect one mediated by the release of lipoxygenase products (Dallob et al., 1987). Whether these metabolites have a role as hyperalgesic mediators in dental pain remains to be determined.

### 26.5.3 Interaction with other chemical mediators

Besides eicosanoids, some endogenous chemical mediators have been implicated in pulp inflammatory process and the pain often associated with inflammation. A local thermal insult on porcine dental pulp causes a fourfold increase in pulpal histamine level (DelBalso et al., 1976). Inoki et al. (1979) have identified bradykinin as an endogenous painproducing substance released into the dental pulp by application of various noxious stimuli. Since PGs potentiate the pro-inflammatory activity of chemical mediators like 5-HT, histamine or bradykinin (Moncada and Vane, 1979), these mediators may interact with each other in the pulp inflammatory process. Interestingly, local application of compound 48/80, known to release endogenous active substancessuch as histamine, 5-HT and eicosanoids from mast cells into dentinal cavities causes a nerve activity and functional loss of blood flow (Ahlberg and Edwall, 1977).
