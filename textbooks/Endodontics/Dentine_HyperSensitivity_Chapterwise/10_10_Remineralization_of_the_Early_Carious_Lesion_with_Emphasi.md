### 10.1

### Introduction

According to Olley and Bartlett (Chap. 4),
Erosion (acid dissolution) is the most common and important etiological factor for tooth wear globally as well as important etiological factor in the development of Dentine Hypersensitivity (see
West et al. 2013). These processes occur due to the superficial demineralization of hard tissue and the subsequent chemical dissolution of the apatite crystals in enamel following an acidic challenge (Bartlett 2005). It is evident from the published literature that so-called acid erosion is caused by relatively small acidic challenges (sometimes in conjunction with other etiological factures such as attrition and abrasion, aided by dietary intake, etc.) leading to the loss of enamel

and dentine and subsequently exposing the dentine tubules and initiate DH (see Chap. 4).
According to Zero and Lussi (2005), the protective role of a mature salivary pellicle is key in the prevention against an erosive challenge, its removal, therefore may be the first step in the demineralization of the underlying enamel and dentine. It comes as no surprise, therefore that products developed for the treatment of dentine hypersensitivity are also effective in the treatment. These products have been extensively reviewed by Victor Mosquim and colleagues (see
Chap. 7) as well as in Chaps. 5 and 6 and as such this chapter will focus on the available published literature that supports the efficacy of these products in treating demineralization (see Table 10.1).
The products outlined in Table  10.1 are designed to treat several clinical conditions and despite various claims made for the effectiveness in treating the loss of mineral tissue from the tooth surface the consensus from the published reviews such as systematic reviews with/without meta-analysis appears somewhat inclusive for some of the products. Part of the problem as expressed in several reviews is the lack of standardization of both in vitro and in vivo studies and perhaps this should be addressed in the future.
