## Prevention

Fractures are more likely when the tooth structure has been weakened by loss of the hard tissues of the tooth. Therefore primary prevention consists of the maximum possible protection of the hard tissues of the tooth [restoration, shaping of the access cavity). inappropriate use of aggressive bun (Gates or Peeso burs) when shaping the secondary access cavity removes an unnecessary amount of substance, especially at the orifices of the root canals.
Severely weakened teeth are at risk of developing cusp and/or root fractures during the treatment pertod. Therefore, t h e n  treating posterlor teeth, measures that need to be considered include shortening of cusps at risk of ftacture before startingthetreatment and stabilization of the hard tissues of the tooth with adhesive restorations. Such measures are peiformed before the treatment in orderto set clear, reproducible reference polnts.They are of parficular importance ff the treatment period is likely to be prolonged (during pregnancy, in classical apexification therapy).
Since the forces exerted during the lateral condensabion of gutta-percha may lncrease the risk of vertical root fracture^,^^ meticulous care is needed to ensure that fingerspreaders of adequate size and taper are used forthe condensationithat is, spreaders that do not cause friction in the mot canal during condensalton and thus do not act

as levers (see Chapter 73). However, the available data do not provide any evidence against the use of lateral condensation.

In addition, the taper of the prepared root canal can affect the amount of stress on the root during root canal rest0ration.A small taper of 4-6% may lead to considerable stress in the apical region of the root this stress is much reduced when the taper is 12%.16 in contrast, mot canal preparation with instrumentr having greater tapers (12% taper) results in a significant reduction in resistance to fracture?'
Metallic posts can represent a further risk to the stability of the remaining root, because their physical properttes are quite different from those of dentin. The avoidance of metallic posts a recommended for fracture prevention.
Furthermore, any post space preparation leads to considerable loss of tooth substance,~~ thatthe remaining walls ofthe root become rtgnificantly weakened. Premolars in particular tend to have much less root substance left than will be apparent on the radl~graph?~
