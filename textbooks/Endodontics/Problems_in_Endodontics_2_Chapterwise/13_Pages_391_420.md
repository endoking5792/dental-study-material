## Background

Perforation of the dental hard tissues, with the resultant artificlally or iatrogenically created communication between the endodontic system and the oral cavity or the surroundingtissues, may occuratany stage of endodontic treatment. Perforations may also be present prior to start of the endodontic treatment, as a result of previous treatment.
Apart from iatrogentc causes, resorptiie or carious processes can also lead to petforation. The incidence of perforations is 3-1096.1 About half of all Iatrogenic perforations occur during root canal treatment (whik searching for the mot canal orifices, treating calcified mot canals, instrumentingroot canals), while the other half occur during restorative procedures (post and core  preparation^).^

## Nature of the problem

Every perforation represents an injury to the integrity of the tooth or Its root, and thus of the periodontal tissue. A perforation can a d  as a pathway for the spread of infection from the interior of the root canal to the periodontium, initiating inflammatory osteolysis.The converse is also posslbie (1.e. the endodontic system may become infeded via a perforation) if a communication to the oral cavity Is established via the periodontal ligament space, Most iatrogenic perforations are not identified Immediately or are not adequately treated. in particular, dentists often fail to identi@ a perforation risk, or fail to rule out the existence of a perforation, while canying out procedures that carry a high risk of perforation (eg. during post space preparation).

The goal of perforation repair, in addition to the elimination of microbes, must be to restore the integrity of the root by means of a tight, biocompatible seal, and thus to facilitate regeneration of the damaged periodontal tissue.
