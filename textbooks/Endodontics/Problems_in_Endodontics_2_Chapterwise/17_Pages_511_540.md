## Fracture of the tooth during or after bleaching

## Nature of the problem

Considerable importance is attached to the question of whether bleaching makes the hard tissues of the tooth more briffle, resulting in reduced fracture resistance.

Unfortunately, the literature contatns few studies on this subject. It is true that laboratory studies have shown thatthe application of bleaching agents may lead to a slight reduction in the fracture resistance of enameL23*24 However, when dentin Itself was studied, no increase in brittleness was found even when a mixture of sodium perborate with 30% hydrogen peroxide was usedT5 Consequently, no fractures of the whitened teeth were observed in1;ny of the clinlcal studies investigating internal bleaching. Nevertheless, it should be noted thatthe tooth may become considerably weakened by the removal of the discolored dentin. An increased fracture risk is particularly likety if the tooth has already been weakened by marked loss of hard tissue in the past (caries, excavation).

### Prevention

if tooth fractures are to be prevented, only highly discolored dentin should be removed, preserving as much of the hard tissue ofthe tooth as possible. Teeth that have undergone a lot of restoration and considerable loss of hard tissue should not be bleached. The risk Is particularly hi@ during the walking bleach phase, when the tooth is not Internally stabilized by an adhesive restoration: therefore, particular care is needed to ensure correct occlusion of the temporary seal of the access caviq.

Fracture of the complete crown of the tooth, or partial fracture.
