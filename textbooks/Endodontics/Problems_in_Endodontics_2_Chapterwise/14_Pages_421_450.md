## Definition

Resorption is defined as the physiological or pathological reabsorption of dental tissue and secreted substances.

## Background

As resorption often results from chronic, long-term inflammation, it is often diagnosed incidentally. Resorption may, but need not, be self-limlting.

## Nature of the problem

If not diagnosed and treated in good time, resorption may lead to extensive loss of hard tooth structure, eventually resulting in loss of the tooth. The cavities and lacunae in the

root which are caused by resorption are only partly accessible to adequate treatment.

## Etiology

Dentin is protected on its inner side by predentin and the odontoblast layer, and on its outer side by precementum and the cementoblasts. Inflammatory resorption occurs when the predentin or precementum become mineralized orwhen the precementum suffers mechanical damage. As a result, the denuded surfaces may be colonized and resorbed by multinuciear cells (the cementoclasts and odontoclasts).As long as mineralized root tissue is covered by predentin, precementum and adontoblasts or cementoblasts, protecting it from colonization by macrophages, osteoclasts and giant cells, the

root will also be protected from resorption.

### Replacement resorption

Replacement resorption occurs particularly following traumatic tooth injuries (especially after avulsion and longer periods of extraoral storage of the teeth in non-physiological solutions/media). The severe root luxation leads to extensive necrosis of the periodontal ligament, followed by bone deposition directly onto the denuded root surface. As a result of the direct communication between bone and root, the root is included in the physiological bone remodeling processes, with eventual complete resorption of the

root and its "replacement" by bone (Fig. 19-30).

Dlagnosis
I
Percussion: Metallic percussion sound.
Mobility: No tooth mobility.

Radlographlc diagnosis: Absence of the periodontal ligament space, the external root contours disappear progressively, taking on a "mott-eaten" appearance.
Pulp sensitivity testing: Positive.

Management

No promising form of treatment is known at present.
