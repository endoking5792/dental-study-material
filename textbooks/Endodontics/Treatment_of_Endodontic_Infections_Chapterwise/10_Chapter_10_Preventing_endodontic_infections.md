As root canal infection is the primary cause of apical periodontitis, measures must be taken to prevent the introduction of microorganisms into the canal during and after treatment. Prevention is essential when treating both noninfected (vital) and infected teeth (regardless of whether they are necrotic or require retreatment).

Key measures for the prevention of infection include:

Asepsis during treatment; and
Promotion of an adequate antibacterial coronal seal between appointments and after the conclusion of the endodontic treatment.

If microorganisms are introduced into the canal after professional intervention, a secondary infection may occur. Non-oral bacteria have been isolated from root canals in certain cases, and their occurrence is a clear indication of secondary infection. Non-oral bacterial species already detected in canals include Pseudomonas aeruginosa,3,13,14,18

Staphylococcus epidermidis,1,6,11,15-17,19-22
Staphylococcus xylosus,1,17,19,20
Bacteroides fragilis,2,7,8,12,22
Klebsiella pneumoniae,5,15 and Enterobacter cloacae.3,5,18,19

As so-called “nonsurgical” endodontic treatment is actually a microsurgical approach, a strict aseptic technique must always be followed throughout the procedure.

The “Ten Commandments” for the prevention of endodontic infections are:

Remove dental plaque and calculus from the tooth to be treated and at least from the two immediately adjacent teeth before rubber dam placement. Based on current knowledge of bacterial involvement in apical periodontitis development, this should never be neglected.
Isolate the affected tooth with rubber dam. Rubber dam isolation is of the utmost importance for maintaining the aseptic chain as it makes it possible to disinfect the operative field and prevents contamination by saliva during endodontic procedures. Due to

rubber dam isolation, few if any medical and dental surgical procedures can be performed as aseptically as endodontic treatment. Consequently, it comes as no surprise that the survival rate of root canal-treated teeth is significantly increased when rubber dam isolation is utilized in comparison with when it is not.9,10 Also, the use of rubber dam during post placement positively impacted the success of root canal-treated teeth.4

Moreover, rubber dam isolation increases visibility, protects the patient from accidentally swallowing or aspirating instruments, and protects the oral mucosa from the caustic effects of substances used during endodontic procedures. If rubber dam placement is not possible, neither is endodontic treatment.
Clean the operative field, including the tooth crown, clamp, and rubber dam with 3% to 6% hydrogen peroxide and disinfect with one of the following solutions: 2.5% sodium hypochlorite (NaOCI), 2% chlorhexidine, or 2% iodine solution (Fig 10-1). This is done by soaking cotton or gauze with the solution and applying it in a centrifugal direction (starting with the tooth crown).
Completely remove all caries remnants and defective restorations before placing any instrument in the canal. These potential sources of residual bacteria can contaminate the canal.
Place only sterile instruments in the canal. Endodontic instruments that are not available sterilized from the manufacturer must be made sterile in an autoclave before use.
Never touch the part of the instrument that will be introduced into the canal. This causes a breach in the aseptic chain, contaminating the instrument with bacteria and debris present on the surfaces of the gloves. Even when using sterile surgical gloves, touching the endodontic instrument should be avoided to protect the clinician from accidental cross-infection when handling an instrument that was just removed from the canal.
